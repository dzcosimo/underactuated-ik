Pushing the boundaries of actuators-to-task kinematic inversion: from fully actuated to underactuated (soft) robots

Cosimo Della Santina
Department of Cognitive Robotics, Delft University of Technology

In short

An underactuated robot cannot hold every configuration. Inverse kinematics therefore has to find a configuration that reaches the target and that the robot can actually hold. The paper gives two closed-loop algorithms that do both, with conditions under which they converge.

Abstract

Kinematic inversion via closed-loop schemes is at the core of the success of fully actuated robotic manipulators. These algorithms robustly and efficiently compute a reference for low-level controllers to position the end effector or other selected parts of the robot's body in space. This paper concerns itself with the extension of kinematic inversion algorithms beyond fully actuated systems into the realm of underactuated robotics. We propose two strategies that address this challenge under different assumptions. The first is obtained by revisiting a well-known kinematic inversion algorithm from continuum robotics, the direct task-to-actuators inversion, and reformulating it so that it generalizes beyond that field and beyond its current strong assumptions, including full actuation. We then introduce a second closed-loop inversion algorithm that applies in even more general conditions, no longer requiring explicit solutions of the robot's statics. This is achieved by showing that the configuration feasibility constraint can be expressed as a holonomic one and embedding it within the inverse kinematics loop in the manner of constrained CLIK.

Explainer 1 · 1.5 min

The problem

With a motor on every joint, inverse kinematics is geometry: any configuration that reaches the target works, because the motors hold it. Take away one motor and gravity and springs decide where the passive joint goes. Most configurations that reach the target can no longer be held. The ones the robot can hold form a set \(\mathcal{E}\), and the solution has to lie on it.

Explainers 2 and 3

Two ways to stay on \(\mathcal{E}\)

Solve the statics first

Fix the actuators and the robot settles in one held configuration. Write that as \(q_{\mathrm u} = \bar q_{\mathrm u}(q_{\mathrm a})\). The result is a square map from actuators to task, defined on held configurations only, and classic closed-loop IK runs on it.

\(\dot q_{\mathrm a} = J_{h\circ h_{\mathrm a}}^{-1}\,\Gamma\,\big(\bar x - h\circ h_{\mathrm a}(q_{\mathrm a})\big)\)

Needs
an explicit solution of the statics, \(\bar q_{\mathrm u}(q_{\mathrm a})\)
Gives
a light loop in \(m\) dimensions; every intermediate shape is held
Watch the full explainer (3 min, includes a side note for soft roboticists)

Make the statics a constraint

When \(G_{\mathrm u}=0\) cannot be solved for \(q_{\mathrm u}\), treat it as a second task. Ask both the task error and \(G_{\mathrm u}\) to decay exponentially; at each step that gives a linear system in \((\dot q_{\mathrm a}, \dot q_{\mathrm u})\).

\(\dot q = P_{\mathcal E} J_{\mathrm a}^{-1}\Gamma_Z(\bar x - h) - P_Z H_{\mathrm{uu}}^{-1}\Gamma_E G_{\mathrm u}\)

Needs
gradients of \(h\) and \(G_{\mathrm u}\); \(J_{\mathrm a}\) invertible
Gives
works from any start, no case-by-case derivation; heavier per step

Side note

If you work in soft robotics

The actuator, configuration and task spaces of continuum robotics, with an actuation model between the first two, may look familiar. That picture gives the three spaces the same dimension, so the actuators alone fix the configuration.

In reality the configuration space is much larger. The held configurations form a set \(\mathcal{E}\) with the dimension of the actuators, and the actuators-to-task map lives on it. Without gravity and with uniform segments, \(\mathcal{E}\) is exactly the flat sheet of the familiar picture. That is a good reason why the fully actuated approximation has worked so well in practice.

Practitioner's guide

Using the algorithms on your robot

1. Match the math to your robot

\(q\)
the full configuration: joint angles, curvatures, shape parameters
\(q_{\mathrm a}\)
actuated coordinates: motor angles, tendon lengths, chamber volumes
\(q_{\mathrm u}\)
what still moves if you block the actuators: passive joints, curvature differences within a segment
\(c(q)\)
the map \(q \mapsto (q_{\mathrm a}, q_{\mathrm u})\); in practice choose \(q_{\mathrm a}\) from the actuators and add enough \(q_{\mathrm u}\) to fix the shape
\(x = h(q)\)
the task: tip position, pose, centre of mass, contact point
\(G(q)\)
potential forces (elastic, gravitational): what remains of the dynamics after removing all terms in \(\ddot q\), \(\dot q\) and \(\tau\)
\(G_{\mathrm u}\)
the part of \(G\) along \(q_{\mathrm u}\): forces the actuators cannot counteract. Held configurations satisfy \(G_{\mathrm u}=0\)

2. Pick the strategy

Can you solve \(G_{\mathrm u}(q_{\mathrm a}, q_{\mathrm u}) = 0\) for \(q_{\mathrm u} = \bar q_{\mathrm u}(q_{\mathrm a})\), and evaluate it quickly?

  • Yes: solve the statics first. Simpler, cheaper per step, easier to tune.
  • No: make the statics a constraint. More general and needs no case-by-case derivation, at the price of more computation and more sensitivity to poor conditioning.

3. Practical remarks

  • Near singularities, use a damped inverse in place of a plain one, with either strategy.
  • If \(c^{-1}\) is hard to derive symbolically, integrate \(\dot q\) from \((\dot q_{\mathrm a}, \dot q_{\mathrm u})\) using the inverse Jacobian of \(c\).
  • \(\partial G_{\mathrm u}/\partial q_{\mathrm a}\) and \(\partial G_{\mathrm u}/\partial q_{\mathrm u}\) can be approximated by finite differences.
  • The loop produces references for the actuated coordinates; a low-level controller tracks them. For a stable enough system, a PID on the actuator side suffices.
  • Start "statics as a constraint" from a held or nearly held configuration, for example the robot's current one. It converges much faster.

Solve the statics first

define  q̄u(qa)          explicit equilibrium map
        f(qa) = h(c⁻¹(qa, q̄u(qa)))
        J(qa) = ∂f/∂qa
choose  Γ ≻ 0, tolerance εx, step Δt, initial qa
while ‖x̄ − f(qa)‖ > εx
    ex ← x̄ − f(qa)
    qa ← qa + Δt · J(qa)⁻¹ Γ ex
    qu ← q̄u(qa)

Make the statics a constraint

define  f(qa, qu) = h(c⁻¹(qa, qu))
        Ja, Ju       task Jacobians
        Hua, Huu     derivatives of Gu
choose  ΓZ, ΓE ≻ 0, tolerances, step Δt, initial (qa, qu)
while ‖x̄ − f‖ > εx  or  ‖Gu‖ > εE
    ex ← x̄ − f,   eE ← −Gu
    PE ← [I; −Huu⁻¹Hua] (I − Ja⁻¹Ju Huu⁻¹Hua)⁻¹
    PZ ← [−Ja⁻¹Ju; I] (I − Huu⁻¹Hua Ja⁻¹Ju)⁻¹
    (qa, qu) ← (qa, qu) + Δt · (PE Ja⁻¹ΓZ ex + PZ Huu⁻¹ΓE eE)
